Three ways to measure platform performance

A rating is only one part of the picture.

The research behind this tool shows that being seen, being rated highly and attracting interaction are related but different outcomes. The same restaurant characteristic can relate to them very differently.

How it works

Four short steps, about three minutes.

  1. 1Describe your restaurantCity, locality, price tier and cuisine. Only the first three are required.
  2. 2Add what you knowYour current rating, votes and services, if you want those comparisons.
  3. 3Choose your prioritiesVisibility, rating, engagement, price positioning or local benchmarking.
  4. 4Get your diagnosticLocality, city and NCR benchmarks, similar-restaurant peers, gaps and up to five suggestions, plus a printable report.
Start the diagnostic →

Or open a synthetic example (an invented profile, not a real restaurant).

What you get

Comparisons first. Evidence always visible.

  • Benchmarks in a fixed order: your locality, your city, all of Delhi NCR, then similar restaurants.
  • Exact sample sizes. Every comparison shows how many restaurants it rests on, and is hidden when there are too few.
  • Plain language first. Regression estimates, confidence intervals and p-values are one click away.
  • No invented scores. The tool never grades your restaurant or calculates a health score.

Before you start

Historical data. Benchmarks come from one undated Zomato listing snapshot. They are research references, not current Zomato performance.

Unverified entries. What you enter is not checked against live Zomato data. The historical data may or may not include your own restaurant.

Associations, not causes. The study is observational. No suggestion is a guaranteed improvement.

    About, privacy and limitations

    NCR Restaurant Intelligence applies the findings of the research project “Visible, Liked, or Both? Understanding Restaurant Visibility, Perceived Quality and Customer Engagement on Zomato”.

    Privacy statement. This website has no accounts, no login, no backend, no database, no analytics and no third-party services. It makes no network request except to load its own files. Your restaurant profile is processed in this browser tab only and is kept in session storage until you clear it or close the tab. The tool does not collect email addresses, phone numbers, customer names or any personal information.

    Visible, liked, or both?

    How restaurant visibility, perceived quality and customer engagement vary across Delhi NCR.

    This is the research evidence behind the owner diagnostic. A rating is only one part of performance on a discovery platform, so the study measures three outcomes separately and compares them across five cities and the City + Locality units of Delhi NCR.

      Why the three outcomes are distinct

      The same restaurant characteristics appear in all three models, but the size and even the direction of their associations differ. Chain status is the clearest case: chain outlets were more visible and more engaged, but rated lower.

      Filled: statistically significant (p < .05). Hollow: not significant. Baseline specifications; engagement among rated restaurants. Source: final paper, Table 10.

      Who has a published rating?

      In this dataset, every listing with 0–3 votes shows a rating of 0 and every listing with 4 or more votes has a rating. That is consistent with a publication threshold, but it is not confirmed Zomato policy. A zero means “no published rating”, never zero quality.

      Research explorer · Localities

      Benchmark a City + Locality unit

      Choose a city, then a locality, and compare it with its city and with all of Delhi NCR. Localities are identified as City + Locality, because some names (for example “MG Road” and “Sector 15”) occur in more than one city.

      What this shows. Localities differ far more than cities. Replacing city with locality in the models roughly doubled the share of rating variation accounted for (0.131 to 0.235). Coverage is concentrated in New Delhi, and individual ranks are indicative because they move when the eligibility threshold changes.

      How the top and bottom performers differ

      Each comparison uses its own population. Rated-only and all-NCR comparisons are shown separately and never pooled.

      What this shows. The highest-rated restaurants were set apart by price positioning and by being independent rather than chains, not by delivery. The most-voted restaurants were set apart by delivery, upper-mid pricing and broader menus. Top-voted is not the same as best.

      Research explorer · Findings

      What is associated with each outcome?

      Three regression models share one specification: online delivery, table booking, price tier, cuisine breadth, chain status and city fixed effects. Each estimate is an association, holding the other characteristics constant.

      How to read the charts. Visibility uses odds ratios (1 = no difference; 2 = twice the odds, not twice the probability). Perceived quality uses rating points (0 = no difference). Engagement uses percentage differences in the geometric mean of (1 + Votes), which is not an exact percentage change in raw votes. Filled dots are statistically significant; lines are 95% confidence intervals. Glossary

      Price tier: the Tier 2 to Tier 3 step

      Location adds explanatory power

      Adjusted R² (share of variation the model accounts for in this sample) with city vs locality fixed effects (98 City + Locality units; pseudo-R² for visibility). Locality effects bundle footfall, demographics, rents and restaurant mix; they are not effects of location.

      Direction of association across outcomes and extremes

      + / −: significant positive / negative association (p < .05); (ns): not significant. Baseline specifications. The all-NCR engagement column includes unrated restaurants and is a sensitivity model. Source: final paper, Table 10.
