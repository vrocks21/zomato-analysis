NCR Restaurant Benchmark Explorer

A rating is only one part of restaurant performance on a discovery platform. This explorer follows the research project “Visible, Liked, or Both?” and separates performance into three outcomes, so you can see how each one varies across the five cities and the City + Locality units of Delhi NCR.

    Why the three outcomes are distinct

    The same restaurant characteristics appear in all three models, but the size and even the direction of their associations differ. Chain status is the clearest case: chain outlets are more visible and more engaged, but rated lower.

    Filled: significant (p < .05). Hollow: not significant. Baseline specifications; engagement among rated restaurants. Source: final paper, Table 10.

    Who has a published rating?

    In this dataset, every listing with 0–3 votes shows a rating of 0 and every listing with 4 or more votes has a rating. That is consistent with a publication threshold, but it is not confirmed Zomato policy. A zero means “no published rating”, never zero quality.

    Research findings

    What is associated with each outcome?

    Three regression models share one specification: online delivery, table booking, price tier, cuisine breadth, chain status and city fixed effects. All estimates are associations, holding the other characteristics constant.

    Price tier: the Tier 2 to Tier 3 step

    Location adds explanatory power

    Adjusted R² with city fixed effects vs locality fixed effects (98 City + Locality units; pseudo-R² for visibility). Locality effects bundle footfall, demographics, rents and restaurant mix; they are not effects of location.

    Direction of association across outcomes and extremes

    + / −: significant positive / negative association (p < .05); (ns): not significant. Baseline specifications. The all-NCR engagement column includes unrated restaurants and is a sensitivity model. Source: final paper, Table 10; RESULTS/tables/integrated_direction_table.csv.
